MLMP trace explorer

Two planners, one scene, one seed, replayed from committed mlmp.traceviz/1 recordings. Solution motion plays the arms along the solution, interpolated in joint space the way the planner's own motion validator checked it. Search tree shows what the planner explored to find it — read it one bundle level at a time; every dot is one robot's chain tip at that vertex's level, and a level-L vertex owns only its first L+1 links.

The lower canvas is the real configuration space of the scene's 2-D bundle level: each axis is one joint angle over [−π, π], the grey region is where the arms collide, and the tree is drawn in the space the planner actually searches. Higher levels are drawn faintly in the same square because a bundle level is the projection of the one above it. How much of that square is blocked depends on the scene: scene2's level 1 is a two-link arm with 38% of its configuration space inside an obstacle, while scene3's level 0 is two short stubs that can barely reach one.

Both panels of a scene get the same seed and the same budget, and the budget differs by scene (10 s or 60 s) because recording costs planner time: every trace frame re-dumps the whole graph, so a traced run explores less than an untraced one at the same wall clock. In these recordings BiQRRT solves scene1 and scene2; QRRT solves nothing, and scenes 3 to 6 go unsolved for both. Collision checking became roughly two orders of magnitude finer when a resolution defect was fixed, which is why fewer cells solve here than this project's older numbers claim — those paths were cheaper because their interiors were barely checked.
